The line is old. Thomas Watson Jr., IBM, 1973. Dieter Rams never said it quite like that, but his work lives by it. Today it sounds almost embarrassing — too often on T-shirts, too often repeated on conference stages.

Even so: it holds.

## What Rams would mean today

Rams formulated ten principles for good design. Translated to brands — not products — they read as follows:

1. **A brand is innovative only when it does something.** Not when it follows a trend.
2. **A brand is useful.** It helps customers decide, employees communicate, the company recognise itself.
3. **A brand is aesthetic.** Not because beauty is worth something — but because ugly costs something.
4. **A brand makes a product understandable.** It's the explanation that always runs alongside.
5. **A brand is unobtrusive.** It doesn't shout. It works.
6. **A brand is honest.** It promises nothing more than it keeps.
7. **A brand is long-lasting.** It is cared for over decades, not refreshed every three years.
8. **A brand is consistent.** It makes no exceptions, because otherwise it stops existing.
9. **A brand is resource-friendly.** It needs less explanation because it's already there.
10. **A brand is as little brand as possible.** It steps back as soon as the product or service does the work.

## Why this matters today

In a time when anyone can produce "brands" in minutes, substance becomes the differentiator. A brand that does justice to Watson and Rams is a business decision with leverage. It makes acquisition easier, pricing more stable, employee loyalty stronger.

It is good business. Today still.
