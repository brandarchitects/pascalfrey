Geberit is Swiss industry with global reach. The brief: structure the brand architecture across multiple product lines and equip marketing teams worldwide with consistent material — without tying their hands in their markets.

## The starting point

Over the years, different visual expressions had grown across product lines and regions. What was defined consistently at headquarters was interpreted freely in markets. The result: a recognisable logo, but variable application.

## The approach

We structured the brand architecture in three layers: parent, product lines, regional application. Each layer got clear rules and its own template set. Headquarters maintains central templates; markets adapt along a clearly defined axis.

## The outcome

Marketing teams in over 40 countries now work with a clear template structure. Consistency has measurably improved. So has speed of adaptation — because there's less improvising.

## What I take from it

- Brand architecture isn't a graphic task. It's governance.
- Templates work when they are narrower than the wishlist of the markets.
- International consistency is always a political project.
